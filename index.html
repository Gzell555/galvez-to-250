From Gálvez to America250

Spain’s role in American independence, researched and written by the students of Fairfax County

Bernardo de Gálvez, the Spanish governor of Louisiana, supplied the Patriots and drove the British from the lower Mississippi and the Gulf Coast. In 2025, the Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest, developed by the Langley World Affairs Club with the Embassy of Spain, invited every middle and high school student in Fairfax County Public Schools to research his story and Spain’s part in the founding of the United States.

What might students find if they took the time to investigate the story for themselves?

Seventy-one students from 16 schools answered. This page tells the history they studied, how the contest reached them, and what they wrote, as the United States marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence.

1776 to 1783

Spain and the American Revolution

Bernardo de Gálvez became governor of Spanish Louisiana in 1777. From New Orleans, before Spain had entered the war, he allowed gunpowder, muskets, medicine, and cloth to move up the Mississippi River to American forces, working with the American agent Oliver Pollock to supply George Rogers Clark’s campaign in the Illinois country.

Spain declared war on Great Britain in June 1779. That fall, Gálvez led a force of Spanish regulars, Louisiana militia, free Black companies, and Choctaw allies that took the British posts at Manchac, Baton Rouge, and Natchez. In March 1780 he captured Mobile. In 1781 he laid siege to Pensacola, the capital of British West Florida. When the Spanish fleet hesitated to enter the bay under British guns, Gálvez sailed in first aboard the brig Galveztown, and the fleet followed. Pensacola surrendered on May 8, 1781.

By the end of the campaign, Britain had lost the lower Mississippi and the Gulf Coast, and no longer held a base on the Gulf from which to strike at the southern states. The same year, funds raised in Spanish Havana helped pay for the French fleet that sailed to the Chesapeake before the victory at Yorktown.

Gálvez took the motto “Yo solo,” “I alone,” for his coat of arms in memory of Pensacola. Galveston, Texas, bears his name, his portrait hangs in the U.S. Capitol, and in 2014 Congress made him an honorary citizen of the United States.

Gálvez does not replace the figures students already know from the Revolution. He adds another dimension to the history.

February 2025

A Statue, a Question, and a Contest

The contest began at the Embassy of Spain in Washington, D.C. On February 14, 2025, Ryan Minton passed the bronze statue of Gálvez at the entrance on his way inside. There, officials Carla Cabrera Cuadrado and Antonio García Roger introduced him to Gálvez and to Spain’s role in the Revolution, a part of the history he had not yet studied.

Rather than simply share what he had learned, he designed a way for other students to learn it for themselves. Through the Langley World Affairs Club, the Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest invited students in grades 7–12 to research Gálvez, examine Spain’s role in American independence, and reach their own conclusions from the evidence.

“My hope is that other students will come to know this important patriot, just as readily as they know figures like Paul Revere.”

Ryan Minton, March 2025

Spring 2025

Reaching Every School in the County

Members of the Langley World Affairs Club took the contest to students, teachers, librarians, school administrators, and Fairfax County School Board members across the county.

Teachers received materials to bring Gálvez into their classrooms. Contest posters went up in Fairfax County Public Library branches. A social media series introduced Gálvez, the Gulf Coast campaign, and the Siege of Pensacola to students before they began their research. The aim was to introduce the subject and give students a reason to investigate it, not to tell them what to conclude.

Spring 2025

What the Students Wrote

Seventy-one students from 16 Fairfax County schools, in every eligible grade from 7 to 12, submitted essays. Five were written in Spanish.

They wrote biographies, strategic analyses, narrative scenes from the Siege of Pensacola, and essays on the relationship between Spain and the United States since 1783. Many traced the supply route up the Mississippi, the campaigns from Baton Rouge to Pensacola, and the story behind “Yo solo.”

“To look deeper into his contribution, it’s imperative that we ask a critical question: How does one person tip the scales of a full-blown war?”

September 6, 2025

The Students Take the Stage

On September 6, 2025, students, families, educators, school leaders, and representatives of the Embassy of Spain gathered at the Spanish Cultural Center in Washington, D.C., for the inaugural Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest awards ceremony.

The students had done what the contest asked: they had taken a historical question, conducted their own research, and developed their own understanding of Gálvez and Spain’s role in American independence. Their work was recognized by the Ambassador of Spain and by the leadership of Fairfax County Public Schools.

October 28, 2025

Featured at State of Our Schools

On October 28, 2025, Fairfax County Public Schools, the 12th-largest school system in the United States, featured the Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest at State of Our Schools, its largest annual program, before hundreds of staff, students, and community leaders. It was one of three initiatives featured and the only one led by students.

The audience watched Ambassador Ángeles Moreno’s message about the contest. Ryan then joined Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid on stage to discuss how the contest began and what students had learned about Gálvez and Spain’s role in the Revolution.

America at 250

Where It Began, and Where It Goes Next

The statue of Bernardo de Gálvez still stands at the entrance to the Embassy of Spain. In February 2025, it prompted a single question. By the end of that year, 71 students from 16 Fairfax County schools had researched the man it honors, the Embassy of Spain had recognized their work, and the county had presented the contest at its largest annual event.

The Gálvez contest also became a model. Following an introduction from the Embassy of Spain, the Langley World Affairs Club met with the Delegation of the European Union to the United States on November 4, 2025. That conversation became Alliances That Sustain America, an America250 essay contest launching in 2026 that applies the same approach to the partnership between Europe and the United States.
