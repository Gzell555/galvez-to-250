From Gálvez to America250

How one discovery at the Embassy of Spain grew into a countywide student project and a larger question about America’s alliances

On February 14, 2025, I visited the Embassy of Spain in Washington, D.C. with about 50 high school students from around the country. When Antonio García Roger asked how many of us had heard of Bernardo de Gálvez, not one hand went up. Mine stayed down too. I had studied the American Revolution for years, and honestly, I was embarrassed that I had never heard his name.

That night, I started reading about him for myself. But simply telling other students about Gálvez did not seem like enough. I wanted them to have their own discovery, the way I had.

What might students find if they took the time to investigate the history for themselves?

That question became the Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest, developed by the Langley World Affairs Club with the Embassy of Spain. Seventy-one students from 16 Fairfax County schools were recognized for their work, and the contest became the starting point for a larger question about the alliances that shaped the United States.

By Ryan Minton Founder and President, Langley World Affairs Club Langley High School, Class of 2027
1776 to 1783

Spain and the American Revolution

Bernardo de Gálvez became governor of Spanish Louisiana in 1777. From New Orleans, before Spain had entered the war, he allowed gunpowder, muskets, medicine, and cloth to move up the Mississippi River to American forces, working with the American agent Oliver Pollock to supply George Rogers Clark’s campaign in the Illinois country.

Spain declared war on Great Britain in June 1779. That fall, Gálvez led a force of Spanish regulars, Louisiana militia, free Black companies, and Choctaw allies that took the British posts at Manchac, Baton Rouge, and Natchez. In March 1780 he captured Mobile. In 1781 he laid siege to Pensacola, the capital of British West Florida. When the Spanish fleet hesitated to enter the bay under British guns, Gálvez sailed in first aboard the brig Galveztown, and the fleet followed. Pensacola surrendered on May 8, 1781.

By the end of the campaign, Britain had lost the lower Mississippi and the Gulf Coast, and no longer held a base on the Gulf from which to strike at the southern states. The same year, funds raised in Spanish Havana helped pay for the French fleet that sailed to the Chesapeake before the victory at Yorktown.

Gálvez took the motto “Yo solo,” “I alone,” for his coat of arms in memory of Pensacola. Galveston, Texas, bears his name, his portrait hangs in the U.S. Capitol, and in 2014 Congress made him an honorary citizen of the United States.

Gálvez does not replace the figures students already know from the Revolution. He adds another dimension to the history.

February 2025

Turning Discovery into Student Scholarship

Ryan had hurried past the bronze statue of Gálvez on his way into the Embassy. Inside, Carla Cabrera Cuadrado and Antonio García Roger introduced the group to Gálvez and to Spain’s role in the Revolution. On his way out, Ryan stopped at the statue again.

Through the Langley World Affairs Club, he created the Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest for students in grades 7 through 12 across Fairfax County Public Schools, a school system of more than 177,000 students just outside Washington, D.C. Students would research Gálvez, examine Spain’s role in American independence, and reach their own conclusions. The point was not to tell them what to think.

“My hope is that other students will come to know this important patriot, just as readily as they know figures like Paul Revere.”

Ryan Minton, March 2025

March 11, 2025

Launched with the Embassy of Spain

Less than a month after that first visit, on March 11, 2025, the Embassy of Spain gave the contest its full support, and it was launched at the Embassy in Washington, D.C. Ambassador Ángeles Moreno Bau encouraged students across Fairfax County Public Schools to take part.

“By inspiring students to explore Gálvez’s impact, your contest plays an important role in deepening historical understanding and appreciation of our shared heritage.”

Dear Mr. Minton,

I am honored to express the Embassy of Spain’s full support for the Bernardo de Gálvez: A Crucial Ally in American Independence essay contest.

Bernardo de Gálvez was a key figure in the American Revolutionary War, whose leadership, bravery and strategic brilliance helped secure victory for the American Patriots. His contributions forged an enduring bond between Spain and the United States, one that continues to shape our strong partnership today. By inspiring students to explore Gálvez’s impact, your contest plays an important role in deepening historical understanding and appreciation of our shared heritage.

We hope this initiative will spark curiosity and engagement with both American and Spanish history. We encourage students across Fairfax County Public Schools to participate, as learning about figures like Gálvez not only enriches historical knowledge but also reinforces the shared values that define Spain-U.S. relations.

Spring 2025

Taking Gálvez Across Fairfax County

Ryan built the outreach campaign through the Langley World Affairs Club, including a press and media kit with ready-to-use emails, newsletter copy, announcements, flyers, and social media posts for teachers, librarians, and administrators to share with students.

English, history and social studies, and Spanish teachers posted the contest in Schoology, announced it in class, and shared it with Spanish Honor Societies. Fairfax County Public Library branches displayed contest materials, and an Instagram series introduced students to Gálvez, the Gulf Coast campaign, and the Siege of Pensacola.

Spring 2025

What Students Discovered

Seventy-one students from 16 Fairfax County schools were recognized for their work, representing every eligible grade from 7 through 12. Five students chose to write their essays in Spanish.

They wrote biographies, strategic analyses, narrative scenes from the Siege of Pensacola, and essays on the relationship between Spain and the United States since 1783. Many traced the supply route up the Mississippi, the campaigns from Baton Rouge to Pensacola, and the story behind “Yo solo.”

Ryan had created the contest hoping other students would discover something for themselves. Reading their essays, he realized he was still discovering things too: connections, arguments, and details he had not anticipated.

“To look deeper into his contribution, it’s imperative that we ask a critical question: How does one person tip the scales of a full-blown war?”

September 6, 2025

The Students Take the Stage

On September 6, 2025, students, families, educators, school leaders, and representatives of the Embassy of Spain gathered at the Spanish Cultural Center in Washington, D.C., for the inaugural Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest awards ceremony.

The students had done what the contest asked: they had taken a historical question, conducted their own research, and developed their own understanding of Gálvez and Spain’s role in American independence. Their work was recognized by Ambassador Moreno and Fairfax County Public Schools Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid.

October 28, 2025

Featured at State of Our Schools

On October 28, 2025, Fairfax County Public Schools featured the Bernardo de Gálvez Essay Contest at State of Our Schools, its largest annual program, before hundreds of students, educators, school leaders, School Board members, and community leaders. It was one of three initiatives featured and the only one led by students.

The audience watched Ambassador Moreno’s message about the contest. Ryan then joined Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid on stage to discuss how the contest began and what students had learned about Gálvez and Spain’s role in the Revolution.

America at 250

From Gálvez to America250

The Gálvez project also raised a larger question for Ryan. Spain’s support for the American Revolution was not merely symbolic. It had real consequences for the war and for the country being born. If an alliance he had barely known about had mattered so much, what other alliances had shaped the United States? And how could students understand alliances before being asked to judge any particular one?

With an introduction from Antonio García Roger and the Embassy of Spain, Ryan met with the Delegation of the European Union to the United States on November 4, 2025. That conversation became Alliances That Sustain America, launched across Fairfax County Public Schools in September 2026, which asks students how the relationship between Europe and the United States has shaped the American story. Award recipients will be recognized at the EU Delegation in Washington, D.C. Like the Gálvez contest, it is not designed to produce agreement. It is designed to produce understanding. Spain was the starting point.

“Every meaningful discovery begins with a thoughtful question.”

Jovita Neliupšienė, Ambassador of the European Union to the United States, September 17, 2026
